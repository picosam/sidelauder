# S1: can Sidelauder bring a chosen instance forward?

Spike of spec §16.1 (and §9.7, the activation ladder), run 2026-09-29 on
macOS 27.0, Apple silicon, with two registered profiles, A and B,
each launched through its own launcher, plus the default instance. Harness:
[`spikes/Sources/SpikeRunner/`](../../spikes/Sources/SpikeRunner/).

**Verdict: pass, on synthetic and on real input.** Every rung of the ladder
worked in 50 of 50 trials, from an app holding no Accessibility grant, and
click-to-front latency is a quarter of the §12 target. The synthetic trials
could not reproduce the refusal cooperative activation exists for, so the
operator then typed in one profile and switched with the real hotkey or a
real click on the rail stand-in: 18 of 18 hotkeys and 12 of 12 clicks
brought the other profile forward ([real input](#real-input-the-operators-hands)).
R1's check passes.

## How the trials were run

Two apps, one binary:

- **SpikeSubject** stands in for Sidelauder in Mode A. It holds **no**
  Accessibility grant, owns a 48 pt activating panel (the rail stand-in) and
  the ⌃⌥1 / ⌃⌥2 hotkeys, and makes every activation call.
- **SpikeRunner** holds the grant. It sets up each trial (the other instance
  in front, SpikeSubject inactive), posts the synthetic click or hotkey, and
  measures from the workspace notifications and the window list.

The split came from the first smoke runs: with the grant, the harness's own
calls could not stand for an un-granted app.

Synthetic input went only to the harness's own windows. A click was posted
only after the window list showed the subject's panel topmost at that point,
a hotkey only once registered, and typed letters only while a SpikeRunner
text window ("the typist") was in front (spec I6).

Each condition alternated targets A and B, 50 trials. "Typed" conditions put
the typist in front and typed eight letters into it just before the trigger,
so the switch starts from an app with recent keyboard input.

## Results

Latency is from the trigger (posted click or hotkey, or the call) to the
target's activation notification, and to its window being the front one.

| Condition (ladder step) | Setup held | Target in front ≤ budget | Activation p50 / p95 | Window in front p50 / p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rail click, then yield and activate (1) | 50 | 50/50 | 18 / 29 ms | 39 / 55 ms |
| Same, typist in front and just typed into | 89 | 89/89 | 16 / 22 ms | 36 / 45 ms |
| Hotkey: activate self, then yield and activate (0 + 1) | 50 | 50/50 | 14 / 25 ms | 38 / 45 ms |
| Same, from the typist | 50 | 50/50 | 21 / 24 ms | 38 / 45 ms |
| Yield and activate with no input behind it (1, cold) | 49 | 49/49 | 2 / 2 ms | 32 / 35 ms |
| Plain `activate`, no yield, no input | 50 | 50/50 | 2 / 5 ms | 32 / 36 ms |
| Both of the above, from the typist | 100 | 100/100 | 2 / 6 ms | 30 / 34 ms |
| Open the profile's launcher (2) | 50 | 50/50 | 333 / 418 ms | 349 / 434 ms |
| AX frontmost and raise (3), granted harness | 100 | 100/100 | 7–8 / 16 ms | 25–29 / 30–32 ms |

Budget: 300 ms for steps 0 to 3, 1,500 ms for step 2 (§9.7). §12's targets
are p50 ≤ 120 ms and p95 ≤ 250 ms for Mode A; every public-API path met them
with room to spare. Step 0 (the subject activating itself on the hotkey)
succeeded 100/100, p50 5–8 ms, p95 13–15 ms. `activate(from:options:)`
returned true in every trial.

The typed rail click ran 100 trials; 11 in its first series were not
posted, because the guard did not find the panel topmost. The rerun posted
all 50. "Setup held" counts only trials whose starting state was right.

## What the trials cannot show

On macOS 27 nothing was refused, not even a plain `activate` from a
background app with no input behind it. That is the case cooperative
activation (§4.2.2) was built to refuse. Two explanations remain:

- the system does not count synthetic HID events as the user working in the
  front app;
- the refusal does not bite on 27 in these conditions.

The trials cannot tell them apart, so they show that the ladder works when
nothing resists it, not that it survives resistance. The deciding check is
the operator typing in one profile and pressing the real hotkey, or
clicking the rail, for the other: `s1-real`, 10 of each. It is listed with
the other operator trials.

## Real input (the operator's hands)

`SpikeRunner s1-real`: the un-granted subject owned ⌃⌥1 (A), ⌃⌥2 (B) and the
teal square, which always targeted the profile not in front. SpikeRunner
posted nothing; it only measured. The operator typed a few letters in A's
message box without sending and pressed ⌃⌥2, clicked in B's window and pressed
⌃⌥1, and so on; then clicked the square right after typing in A.

| Trigger | Trials | Target in front ≤ 300 ms | `activate` allowed | Step 0 OK | Activation p50 / p95 | Window in front p50 / p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real hotkey | 18 | 18/18 | 18/18 | 18/18 | 20 / 23 ms | 42 / 51 ms |
| Real click on the square | 12 | 12/12 | 12/12 | – | 3 / 4 ms | 31 / 35 ms |

Pass was at least 9 of 10 each. The first run stopped at 20 trials with 18
hotkeys and 2 clicks, so a click-only rerun added 10; all 10 came right after
typing in A. The latencies match the synthetic ones within a few
milliseconds. So real input changes nothing on macOS 27: cooperative
activation did not refuse a single switch.

## Found on the way

**Launchers stall when Control is held.** Upstream's launchers are
AppleScript applets, and an applet opened while Control is held stops at its
startup screen instead of running (probe on a throwaway applet: stopped with
Control and with Control-Option held, ran with Option alone or nothing).
A synthetic key event also leaves its modifier flags in the session until
another event clears them, which is how the first smoke runs left two
launchers waiting on that screen.

For the product this is a real hazard. Pressing ⌃⌥3 for a profile that is
not running opens its launcher while Control is still down. Sidelauder must
wait for the modifiers to be released before opening any launcher, bounded
at 2 s, and say so if they stay down. With that rule, step 2 ran 50/50.

**Step 2 shows the previous window again.** In all 50 trials the order was:
the launcher activates, it quits, the previously active profile comes back
to the front (about 150 to 200 ms in), and only then does the target
arrive. Step 2 is a fallback, and this is why it should stay one.

**AX frontmost does nothing after a hide and unhide.** In S2's setup,
setting `kAXFrontmostAttribute` and raising the main window returned success
but brought the instance forward in only 1 of 50 cases where that instance
had just been hidden and unhidden by another app. Step 1 worked in each of
those cases. Step 3 is ordered after step 1 for this reason as well.

## Hotkey defaults (S13, for D-11)

All §6.5 defaults registered with `RegisterEventHotKey` (status 0): ⌃⌥0 to
⌃⌥9, ⌃⌥[, ⌃⌥], ⌃⌥Tab, and the ⌃⌥⇧ digits the harness uses. None of
macOS's enabled system shortcuts on the test Mac uses Control-Option with
those keys. Not checked: Claude Desktop's own menu shortcuts, which would
mean reading Claude's UI, and the documented defaults of Rectangle, Raycast
and Alfred.

## Design consequences

- §9.7 stands as ordered: step 0 then 1 for hotkeys, step 1 for clicks,
  step 2 and 3 as fallbacks. With the real-input check passed, D-03's ruling
  (no private APIs) stands with no case to reopen it.
- New rule for §9.6 and §9.7: never open a launcher while a modifier key is
  held; wait for release, up to 2 s.
- §9.7's success check stays on the activation notification; the return
  value of `activate` is not evidence either way (it was always true).
